About

qsDEM is a Kishino (1988) inspired non-inertial discrete element model for simulating slow geological and environmental processes. This approach is designed for systems with inertial numbers less than 10−2 and satisfies force balance without fictitious forces or accelerations.

Developed and maintained by Meade Group at Harvard.

In distinct element model (DEM), the grain's position is updated by Newton's second law of motion. The grain only knows which way the force is pushing it, which is perfect for modeling fast, high-inertial events like avalanches, but not so much for slow, low-inertial systems like tectonic deformation. To make DEM work for these systems, heavy artificial damping is required to drain the kinetic energy.

Kishino's quasi-static DEM (qsDEM) solves low-inertial systems by removing mass and time entirely. qsDEM replaces the time-integration of Newton's law with a geometric matrix inversion. When a grain experiences an unbalanced net force, qsDEM calculates the forward step (ΔX) required to deform the surrounding constraints to cancel out the unbalanced force. It solves this by using the contact stiffness matrix (S), and the optimal step forward is calculated by multiplying the net force by the inverse of this matrix,

ΔX = α S−1 Fnet

α is a relaxation factor, which is a numerical stability term to prevent overshooting the target.

What exactly is the contact stiffness matrix (S)? This is the summation of stiffness contributions from every constraint it is currently touching.

S = Σc Tc Sc TcT

Intuitively, before the grain moves, the stiffness matrix (S) acts as a map of its local confines, and by multiplying the force by the inverse of the stiffness, the forward step's movement vector ΔX is rotated away from the stiffest boundaries, automatically steering the grain down the optimal path of least resistance.

To see what this looks like, below is a simple demonstration comparing standard dynamic DEM with qsDEM. The blue ball can be dragged anywhere in the boundary, which gravity will act on. Notice how standard DEM causes the net force to drive the grain directly, which requires damping to settle. In qsDEM, the grain does not bounce, as non-inertial systems carry no momentum.
